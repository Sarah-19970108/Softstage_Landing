The problem

Autistic performers cannot tell beforehand whether an open mic's noise or lighting will be manageable.

So they skip events, or they ask someone to check the room first. Either way, they lose chances to perform.

“I would love to go, but I cannot tell if it will be too loud or too bright until I am already inside.”

Invented quote, written for this project. It is not from a real person.

What performers need

Six things the demo does.

  • Show a noise rating (green, amber or red) on each session, with a clear key.
  • Show a lighting rating (one to three bulbs) on each session, with a clear key.
  • Filter by performance type, noise and lighting together, with a clear message when nothing matches.
  • Show each session as a card with an image, location, date, time, performance types and both ratings.
  • Give venues a guide so they rate sessions the same way.
  • Show whether a quiet space is available, with a clear key.

What it is, and how it works in three steps

Softstage is a listing portal: a searchable directory of open mic sessions. Performers browse and filter it. Venues add sessions to it and rate them.

It is made to be used on a phone: one column, large buttons, plain words, and a Calm mode that switches the sparkle off.

  1. 1Browse session cards

    Each card shows where and when the session is, which kinds of performance it takes, and how loud and how bright it will be.

  2. 2Filter by type, noise and lighting

    Choose the performance types you want, then the loudest and brightest you can manage. You can also ask for sessions with a quiet space. Sessions over your limits are hidden.

  3. 3Choose a session and register interest

    Pick a session that fits and read its details before you decide.

How each requirement is met

Every claim here is true of the demo, or it is marked Planned.

Each requirement, how the solution meets it, and whether it is in the demo or planned
Requirement How the solution meets it In the demo or Planned
1. Noise rating with a clear key Every session card shows a green, amber or red pill, and says Low noise, Moderate noise or Lots of noise in words. A noise key at the top of the Performer view explains each colour. If a venue has not supplied a rating, the card says so. In the demo
2. Lighting rating with a clear key Every session card shows one, two or three lit bulbs with a plain label, such as Low light (1 of 3 bulbs). A lighting key explains each level, including that three bulbs can mean flashing or changing light. In the demo
3. Filter by performance type, noise and lighting together Tick the performance types you want, then choose the loudest and brightest you can manage. All three apply at once, and you can add Only show sessions with a quiet space. If nothing fits, a message says so and offers a Clear filters button. While a limit is set, sessions with missing details are hidden, and a note says how many. In the demo
4. Session cards Each session is a card with an illustration, the location, the date, the time, the performance types it accepts, and both ratings. Where the venue has a quiet space, the card also shows the quiet space symbol. In the demo
5. A guide so venues rate sessions the same way The Venue view has a How to rate your session guide that uses the same keys as the Performer view. Organisers add a session there. If they leave a rating blank, a warning badge appears on that session as its date gets closer. In the demo
6. Show whether a quiet space is available A card shows a small symbol of a round face with a finger to the lips, and the words Quiet space, only when the venue has one. Cards without a quiet space show nothing. A Quiet space key explains the symbol, venues tick a box when they add a session, and a filter shows only sessions with a quiet space. In the demo

What exists already

Two real alternatives, and what they leave out for a performer.

Euan's Guide

External site. Checked on 22 October 2026.

A charity-run review site where disabled visitors rate venues for access.

  • Dublin venues are listed, but the information comes from visitor reviews.
  • For example, one Laughter Lounge review from July 2022 covers physical access and says nothing about noise or lighting.
  • It describes venues for audiences, not sessions, and has no filter for performers.

Eventbrite

External site. Checked on 22 October 2026.

A general event platform with an autism-friendly events category.

  • Cards show an image, date, time, location, organiser and lowest price.
  • Results filter by date, price, format and language only.
  • There is no noise, lighting or performance type filter.

The gap: neither one lets a performer filter sessions by noise, lighting and performance type.

How they cope today

Without that information, performers email the venue, send a friend to check, or skip the event.
